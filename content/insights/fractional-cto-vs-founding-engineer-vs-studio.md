Hire a fractional CTO when you have engineers who need leading. Hire a founding engineer when the product is the company and you can offer equity for years. Use a studio when you need a first version built and have nobody technical yet. Most of the confusion comes from mixing these up.

## What each one is

**A fractional CTO** is a senior technical leader who works for you part time, often a few days a month. They make architecture decisions, review work, hire engineers and manage suppliers. They usually do not write much of the code themselves.

**A founding engineer** is an early, usually full-time hire who builds the product and stays with it. They are typically paid a salary plus meaningful equity. A technical co-founder goes further: they own a founder's share of the company and share the risk.

**A studio** is a team you contract to scope, design and build a defined piece of work, such as a first version of the product. You own what they build, and the engagement has an end.

## When a fractional CTO is right

- You already have engineers, or are about to hire them, and nobody senior to lead them.
- You are choosing between suppliers and need someone to judge their proposals.
- Investors or customers are asking technical due diligence questions you cannot answer.

When it goes wrong: a fractional CTO with no engineers to lead often ends up hiring a studio or freelancers anyway. You then pay for the leader and the team.

## When a founding engineer is right

- The product is the company, and it will need continuous work for years.
- You can offer a real share of the company and, ideally, a salary.
- You have time to find the right person. Finding one usually takes months.

Equity matters here. Carta's analysis of thousands of startups found that only 41% of two-founder teams split equity equally, and that a lead founder usually holds the larger share. A technical co-founder will expect founder-level equity; a founding engineer will expect less, with vesting. Get advice before you promise a number.

Y Combinator runs a free Co-Founder Matching platform through Startup School, which is a reasonable place to start looking.

## When a studio is right

- You need something real to show investors or users soon.
- You have no technical team and cannot hire one quickly.
- You want one team to scope, design and build a defined first version.

When it goes wrong: a studio that keeps the code in its own accounts, or that builds something only it can maintain. Insist that the repository and hosting are in your name from day one, and that the work is documented so another engineer can pick it up.

## They are not mutually exclusive

A common, sensible path:

1. A studio scopes and builds a first version while you raise and look for a technical lead.
2. You hire a founding engineer, who takes over the code the studio wrote. This only works if it was written to be handed over.
3. As the team grows, that engineer may become the CTO, or you bring in a fractional CTO to help them.

## A quick way to decide

| Your situation                                     | Start with                                |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Engineers in place, no senior lead                 | Fractional CTO                            |
| Product is the company, can offer equity, can wait | Founding engineer or technical co-founder |
| No technical team, need a first version soon       | Studio                                    |
| Not sure what to build yet                         | A prototype first, from any of the above  |

Whichever you choose, the same three rules protect you: own the code, see working software often, and make sure someone you trust can read what has been built.
